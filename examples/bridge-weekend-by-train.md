# Case study: a four-day bridge by train

Anonymized from a real session.

**Request:** a solo traveler wants a bridge trip from a capital city to two nearby coastal and industrial cities, at a low budget, using no vacation days.

## Constraints discovered

- Friday is a remote day; Monday is a regional holiday (a Sunday holiday moved to Monday). Both facts came from the official regional decree.
- The cheap flight weekend a week earlier had jumped from about 45 to 347; the bridge weekend was 117 by air.

## Comparison

| Mode | Cost | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Train, round trip | about 104 | Evening departure after a full remote day; arrives at 23:02 |
| Flight, round trip | about 117 | 6:40 departure; more transfers; less flexible |
| Train into the second city | 78 out, 62 back | Worse than returning from the first city (39) |

## Decisions

- Train out on Friday evening with a flexible fare (about 6 extra for a free change and partial refund) because the service carried a speed-restriction notice.
- Hostels with free cancellation and dorms of at most 8 beds.
- One night in the first city, two in the second, buses between them bought the week before.
- Split the time by value: the second city is the photographic highlight, so it gets almost two full days.

## Learned

- Aggregator train prices are "from" prices: confirm on the operator.
- Bridges sell out: buses and hostels should be bought early.
- Late arrival needs a hostel with late reception.
- Say clearly that a full-length visit to a museum interior does not fit; offer the alternative split if it matters.

## Deliverables

A markdown purchase list in the traveler's downloads folder with direct links, calendar events for the purchase session with the same links, and an updated trip note.
