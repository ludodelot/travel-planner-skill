# Workflow: from "where should I go?" to a ready-to-buy plan

## 1. Clarify (ask only what you cannot infer)

Collect, in this order, and skip anything the user or their notes already state:

1. **Window**: exact dates, or a range plus a minimum and maximum length.
2. **Budget**: total or per day; what is included (transport, bed, food, activities).
3. **Who and how**: solo or group, pace (nights per base), walking vs. transport, luggage (carry-on only?).
4. **Interests**: photography, architecture, food, nature, nightlife, museums, events.
5. **Constraints**: work days, remote days, holidays, fixed flights, entry documents.
6. **Deal-breakers**: dorm size, free cancellation, late arrivals, early departures, no night transport.
7. **Already visited** and **new places** the traveler wants to prioritize.

If the user has a notes system or a profile, read it first and only ask about gaps. Ask one short question at a time, and give a default you would pick.

## 2. Build the constraint map

Turn the calendar into free windows (see `constraints-and-calendar.md`). Mark which windows cost vacation days. A trip that returns on a working day is not free.

## 3. Generate candidates cheaply

- Start with a small list (3 to 6) from the traveler's notes, flight-deal pages ("anywhere" searches) and local knowledge.
- Use quick, cheap checks per candidate: round-trip price for the exact window and one or two shifted windows.
- Drop a candidate early when it fails a hard constraint (holiday closure, entry rule, price spike).

## 4. Verify the shortlist

For each remaining option confirm: live price and date, baggage, cancellation, transport timings against check-in times, opening hours of key stops, entry requirements, and weather season. See `price-verification.md`.

## 5. Decide and present

Recommend one option, show the runner-up and what would change the choice. Use the format in `results-format.md`.

## 6. Prepare purchases

Produce an ordered checklist with direct links, the refundable and non-refundable split, and the thing to confirm before paying. Buy non-refundable, price-sensitive items first; free-cancellation items can wait but should be secured when availability is scarce.

## 7. Itinerary and calendar

Only after bookings are decided. Walking-friendly clusters, light-aware timing, a rain plan, a budget, and unverified items clearly marked (`itinerary-design.md`, `calendar-export.md`).

## 8. Record and follow up

Update the trip note with what is confirmed, what is pending and what was learned. Set dated reminders for items that change (weather, operator timetables, reopenings).

## Anti-patterns

- Recommending a trip from a stale price.
- Planning a 10-stop day with no buffers.
- Treating an optional item as booked.
- Asking five questions before showing any value.
- Reading every result page into context: extract, then discard.
