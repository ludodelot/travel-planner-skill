# Events and networking near a trip (or at home)

A travel planner is often asked "what is on in my city or at my destination?" Use this triage.

## Triage criteria

1. **Fit**: matches the traveler's field and goals (analytics, marketing, AI, design, photography).
2. **Schedule**: starts after work ends, does not collide with flights or trips, or sits in a free window.
3. **Cost**: free, paid with a known price, or "price not published" (say so; never assume free).
4. **Registration status**: open, approval required, sold out, waiting list. A registration button does not guarantee a free spot.
5. **Effort and value**: hands-on workshops and unconferences beat generic talks for networking.

## Output

| Event | When | Where | Cost | Status | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Name | Date and time | Venue | Free / price / unknown | Open / waitlist / sold out | Apply / optional / skip |

Group by: apply now, optional, conflicts, skipped (with the reason).

## Actions

- Create an all-day event and a timed event for events that need leaving work early.
- Create a dated reminder to register before tickets open or sell out.
- Add optional events with availability "free" so they do not block the calendar.
- Search the organizer's official page for the registration link; if only a search link is available, say so.

## Waiting lists

If tickets are sold out, give the waiting-list route (often a message to the organizers) and draft the message with the traveler's name, company, job title and work email. Do not trust "next batch" dates that fall after the event.

## Pitfalls

- Event aggregators list events that have already passed or were rescheduled.
- Some sessions in a series are technical and fit only part of the audience: pick the ones that match.
- Free events with limited spots need an early registration; check the same day.
