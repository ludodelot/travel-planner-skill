# Entry requirements

Check before buying any ticket. Rules change; always use the official government source and note the date you read it.

## What to check

| Item | Question |
|---|---|
| Authorization | Does the traveler's nationality need a visa, an electronic authorization or neither? |
| Document | Passport required, or is a national ID card enough? Validity beyond the stay? |
| Lead time | How long does approval take? Order it before flights if it takes days |
| Cost | Fee, refundable or not, validity period, linked to which passport |
| Transit | Layovers can trigger separate rules |
| Health or insurance | Only where required; otherwise optional advice |
| Return rules | Onward ticket requirements |

## Example: electronic travel authorization (UK)

Observed on the government site: the authorization is applied for in the official app or on the official website, costs about 20 GBP, is not refundable, usually arrives by email within a day and can take up to three working days, and is valid for two years or until the passport expires. It is linked to the passport used to apply. Citizens of some countries do not need it: confirm the traveler's nationality on the "do not need an ETA" page before paying.

Advice that held up:

- Use the official app or site only. Intermediary sites charge more and do not speed things up.
- Carry the physical passport the authorization is linked to.
- Keep the approval email with the passport.

## How to present it

"Before you buy the flight: you need X. It costs Y, takes up to Z, apply here (official link). I checked it on <date>. Confirm your nationality exemption first."

## Do not

- Assume the rule from memory for a nationality you have not checked.
- Pay or enter passport data for the traveler.
- Store passport numbers or approval references in notes or repos.
