# Price and availability verification

## The rule

A price is only useful with a date, a source and a condition. Always write: `price, source, checked on <date>, what it includes`.

## Labels

| Label | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| `verified` | Seen on the operator or a live results page today for the exact dates | "Round trip 93 EUR, direct, checked today" |
| `from-price` | Aggregator minimum, not a checkout quote | "Train from 65 EUR" |
| `estimate` | Your own estimate, with the basis | "Food about 20 EUR a day" |
| `stale` | Older than a few days | A note from three weeks ago |

## Why stale prices bite

Real examples from planning sessions:

- A weekend round trip noted at about 67 EUR three weeks earlier was 93 to 169 EUR on the day of booking, depending on the days chosen.
- A regional round trip noted at 41 to 52 EUR was 347 EUR for the same weekend a month later, while the neighboring bridge weekend was 117 EUR.
- A "last batch of tickets" announcement was already sold out by the time the user asked.

Treat notes as candidates, not quotes.

## Checklist before recommending a purchase

1. Exact dates and times for both directions.
2. Total price for the traveler's real luggage (carry-on only, cabin bag, checked bag).
3. Fare conditions: refundable, changeable, free cancellation until when.
4. Arrival and departure times against the next leg (check-in hours, last bus, hostel reception).
5. Notices: strikes, speed restrictions, engineering works, delay compensation rules.
6. Entry requirements for the destination.
7. A shifted-date alternative (one day earlier or later) and the price difference.

## Reading price signals

- "Prices are high / normal / low" on a flight page is a relative signal, not a promise.
- "Only N left at this price" on a hostel page is an availability signal: book soon or accept risk.
- Sold-out hints: "99% of properties no longer available" means choose fast.

## Currency and fees

- Confirm the currency of the checkout and any card or payment fee.
- Add known extras: airport transfer, cabin bag, seat selection, city tax, locker.
- Budget a margin (about 10 to 15 percent) for what you cannot know.

## Booking order

1. Non-refundable and price-sensitive items first (low-cost flights, promotional rail fares).
2. Scarce availability next (hostels in a bridge weekend), preferably with free cancellation.
3. Stable items last (local buses, airport shuttles) but not so late that a bridge weekend sells out.
4. Entry authorizations that take days (ETA, visa) first of all.

## What to say when you cannot verify

"I could not open the operator's checkout, so these are aggregator from-prices. Check the final fare before paying; if it exceeds X, use alternative Y."
