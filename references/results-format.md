# How to present results

## Shape of a good answer

1. **The answer first.** One sentence with the recommendation and the main reason.
2. **A small table** with the real numbers (price, time, conditions) and a status label.
3. **What is unverified** in one short list.
4. **The next action** in order, with links.

## Rules

- Recommend, do not survey. Two or three options at most, with a clear pick.
- Show trade-offs in the traveler's terms (euros, hours, vacation days, comfort), not in general travel language.
- Separate `verified`, `from-price` and `estimate`. Say when something was checked.
- Compare against the baseline the traveler remembers ("last month it was 67, today it is 93").
- Be specific about dates and weekdays; mention holidays and work days when they change the cost.
- Give an honest "I could not check X, here is how you check it".
- When the traveler asks "is this OK?" answer yes or no first, then the one caveat that matters.
- Correct yourself plainly when you find an error, and fix it where it was written.

## Comparison table template

| Option | Dates | Price | Time | Conditions | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| A | Fri 17:48 - Mon 17:12 | 104 train | 5 h each way | Changeable fare | Pick |
| B | Flight | 117 | 1 h + transfers | Early start | Runner-up |

## Purchase list template

| # | What | Choice | Price | Refundable | Link |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

Order: entry authorization, non-refundable fares, scarce availability, stable items.

## Length

Short by default. Offer details on request. For long deliverables (itineraries), put the summary table on top and the detail below.

## Tone

Clear, direct, friendly. Avoid filler, avoid hedging that does not help a decision, avoid inventing certainty.

## Closing line

End with the single most useful question or the next step, never with a menu of ten options.
