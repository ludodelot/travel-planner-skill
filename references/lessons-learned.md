# Lessons learned (from real planning sessions)

Each lesson says what happened, what it cost and what to do instead.

1. **Notes lie after three weeks.** Round-trip fares moved from about 67 to 93 to 169 (same trip, different days), and from about 45 to 347 for another weekend. Re-verify before every recommendation.
2. **One day can halve the price.** Moving the return from a Monday holiday to the next remote-work Tuesday took a weekend flight from 169 to 93 and gave a full extra day. Always test +1 and -1 day.
3. **Use the calendar, not the date.** A bridge only exists if the holiday is real for the traveler's workplace: regional decree plus municipal holidays. The cheapest trip is the one that costs no vacation days.
4. **A Sunday holiday moves.** In one region, the November 1 holiday that fell on a Sunday was moved to Monday, creating a bridge nobody had planned for.
5. **Last working day vs. contract end.** A contract end on a holiday means the previous day is the real last working day.
6. **Entry rules first.** A travel authorization for a neighboring country took days and cost about 20 GBP; flights were bought only after confirming it.
7. **Women-only dorm.** The best-rated hostel's cheapest bed was in a women-only room. Read the room name.
8. **Hostelworld without dates.** The city page ignored the date parameters and showed minimum prices. Compare only prices with dates.
9. **Dorm size is a real filter.** The cheapest beds were in 10 to 18-bed rooms, which the traveler did not want.
10. **Train "from" prices.** The aggregator showed a train price; the operator's checkout had fares with different conditions and a notice about delays that are not compensated.
11. **Check-in vs. arrival.** A train arriving at 23:02 needs a hostel with late reception.
12. **Early flight, no sleep.** A 06:00 return was cheaper but forced a night without sleep. It worked because the next day was remote work and an open-return airport bus ran all night. Plan indoor waiting places and confirm luggage storage.
13. **Sold-out events.** A "last tickets" post was sold out within days; the site only offered a waiting list. Draft the message instead of promising tickets.
14. **Do not reuse the traveler's tabs.** Navigating one replaced a page they were using. Open new tabs and name what each holds.
15. **Be careful with "already bought".** A calendar event said a ticket was bought when it was not. Fix wrong claims everywhere they were written.
16. **Prefer commuting-friendly purchase slots.** Schedule purchases when the traveler can actually do them (lunch break, after the commute), with all links inside the event.
17. **Plan for tools failing.** Scripts blocked on some pages; guessed URLs 404; sites returned results without dates. Fall back to reading page text and say what failed.
18. **One question at a time.** Ask the missing fact (phone number, gender field, company) in a short list, with a recommended default.
19. **Summaries beat dumps.** A 50,000-character itinerary pasted by a user needs a structured review: what is good, what to fix, what to do now.
20. **Keep the traveler in control.** Fill, never submit; draft, never send; open, never pay.
