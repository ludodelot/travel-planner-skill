# Budgeting a trip

## Method

1. Fix what is already paid and what is still to buy.
2. Split the rest: transport, accommodation, food, local transport, activities, entry authorizations, insurance, contingency.
3. Use verified prices for fixed items and labeled estimates for the rest.
4. Add a margin of 10 to 15 percent for what cannot be known.
5. Compute the **cost per day** to compare trips of different length.

## Estimates to start from (replace with local data)

| Item | Low-cost range |
|---|---|
| Food, self-catering plus one cheap meal | 20 to 25 per day in southern Europe, 25 to 40 in the UK and Nordics |
| Local transport | 0 when walking; airport transfer 5 to 10 each way |
| Free museums and viewpoints | 0; one paid viewpoint is usually 7 to 12 |
| Hostel dorm | 20 to 45 per night depending on city and season |
| Luggage storage | 3 to 8 per bag |

These are starting points, not quotes. Verify before presenting them as facts.

## Compare in the traveler's terms

| Option | Total | Days | Per day | Vacation days | New country |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|

A cheaper trip that costs a vacation day is often worse than a slightly dearer free one: price the vacation day when it matters.

## Where money is lost

- Baggage fees at low-cost airlines.
- Booking the wrong fare type (non-refundable when plans might move).
- Transport at odd hours (taxi because the last bus left).
- Paid entries that add little to the goal (for a photographer: viewpoints with a free alternative).

## Tracking

Keep a budget table in the trip note: planned, verified, paid. Do not copy references or card data. Note currency and the date of any conversion.
